# Literature and provenance

The bank uses licensed online SVG source masters and method-level cue reconstruction. No
figure or stimulus bitmap is cropped from a publication, and the downloaded SVGs are kept
unchanged under `assets/source/`. Exact file URLs, licenses, creators, and hashes are in
`assets/source/PROVENANCE.md`.

- Hardstone et al. (2022), *Frequency-specific neural signatures of perceptual content and
  perceptual stability*, eLife 11:e78108. The unambiguous control manipulated content,
  outlines, and shading. <https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.78108>
- Wang, Arteaga, and He (2013), *Brain mechanisms for simple perception and bistable
  perception*, PNAS 110:E3350-E3359. The modified face-vase controls produced the intended
  interpretation on 82.7% of trials on average. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1221945110>
- Peterson and Salvagio (2008), *Inhibitory competition in figure-ground perception:
  Context and convexity*. <https://doi.org/10.1167/8.16.4>
- Takashima, Fujii, and Shiina (2012), *Face or Vase? Areal Homogeneity Effect*.
  <https://doi.org/10.1068/p7288>
- Kawabe and Miura (2003), *Figure-ground assignment based on shading*.
  <https://doi.org/10.14947/psychono.KJ00004414308>
- Flounders et al. (2019), *Prestimulus feedback connectivity biases the content of visual
  experiences*. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1817317116>

Hardstone and Wang show disambiguated endpoints in which content, outline/closure, and
shading jointly favor faces or the central object. The present categorical bank includes
isolated and combined cue conditions as method-level hypotheses; it does not claim that
the papers independently tested every axis or that the generated pixels reproduce the
published panels exactly.
